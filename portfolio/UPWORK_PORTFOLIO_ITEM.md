## Description

**A wholesale distributor was ordering stock from a spreadsheet and a gut feeling.
I replaced it with a system that rebuilds itself every night.**

The raw export was a mess — 63,500 transactions, three different date formats in
the same column, five branches spelled 25 different ways, and 13,000 variations
of 40 product names typed by hand at the till. Before anything could be
forecast, the data had to be made trustworthy.

**What I built**

**1. An ETL pipeline that cleans itself.** Parses three date formats without
mixing day and month, collapses the branch spellings, recovers missing prices
from revenue, recomputes revenue where it disagrees with quantity × price, and
separates returns instead of letting them silently cancel sales.

**2. A fuzzy-matching engine for the product names.** 13,000 free-text spellings
resolved to 40 catalogue SKUs — **95% automatically**. The 5% that score below
the confidence threshold go to a review file a human works through. They are
never guessed into the totals. A matching engine that reports 100% is hiding its
mistakes inside the numbers.

**3. A demand forecast that beats the spreadsheet.** LightGBM, next-week units
per SKU, tested on 16 weeks the model never saw:

- **WAPE 24.5%**, against **32.2%** for the best baseline — **23.8% less error**
- Scored against three alternatives the client could do themselves: last week,
  4-week average, same week last year
- Split by time, never randomly, because that is the question the client is
  actually asking

**4. The finding that mattered most.** The strongest input to the forecast was
not sales history — it was the client's own **promotion calendar**, which was
sitting in a spreadsheet connected to nothing. Feeding it in is what took the
error from 32% to 25%.

**5. Three ways to use it, not one.**
- **Dashboard** (Streamlit) — forecast vs actual, model-against-baselines, a
  promotion simulator that re-forecasts live as you change the discount, and a
  data-quality panel that reports every repair the pipeline made
- **API** (FastAPI, Dockerised) — one endpoint, one JSON call; the service keeps
  the history so the caller supplies only the product and the planned discount
- **Telegram bot** — a Monday digest with next week's movers and stock
  shortfalls, plus on-demand answers. The buyer never has to open a dashboard

**Stack:** Python · pandas · RapidFuzz · LightGBM · scikit-learn · FastAPI ·
Streamlit · Plotly · aiogram · Parquet · Docker

**Result:** one command rebuilds the whole chain — ingest, clean, match, train,
evaluate, serve — in about a minute. Full source and the held-out evaluation are
on GitHub; the live dashboard is linked above.

*Data in this demo is synthetic and generated by the repository itself, so it is
fully reproducible and contains no client information.*
